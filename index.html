Prototype rules: four 2-minute quarters; clock runs during live plays; downs, first downs, touchdowns, safeties and possession changes. Touchdowns award 6, followed by a 1-point kick or a single 2-point play. Kickoffs open each half and follow touchdown tries and made field goals. The kicking unit waits at the receiving 40 and the blockers at the receiving 30–35 until the kickoff lands. Direct end-zone kickoffs are touchbacks at the 35; short or sideline kickoffs give possession at the 40. Kickoff returns, meter-controlled punts with returns and playable, unblocked field goals. Field goals: press Space to lock accuracy, keep holding for power, release to kick. Overcharging reduces accuracy; early release reduces range. Kicker Power sets a hard 30–55 yard range cap (ratings 60–99). Accuracy controls window size and meter speed for both teams. Kicker overall averages Accuracy, Power and Stamina. Punt power remains separate. Accuracy windows narrow with distance. Conversion plays are untimed; interceptions end the attempt. 40-second pre-snap play clock; offensive delay of game costs five yards (half the distance near your own goal). Three timeouts per team per half. Punts: F signals a fair catch during flight; touchbacks start at the 20, out-of-bounds and downed punts at the dead-ball spot. Punt catches are automatic when reachable. Punt blocks, muffs, penalties, overtime and franchise saves are not implemented yet. Contact fumbles use Tackling and Protection, with a 0.1–2.5% chance per contact. The hit animates first, the ball falls and bounces, and recovery requires reaching the loose ball. Both teams can recover loose balls. Higher defensive Strength reduces broken tackles and improves contested catches. DB/MLB ratings persist for the game; MLB top speed is 90% of DB speed at the same rating. The fastest rated player is the default returner. Returns use 14% lower top speed and slower acceleration without changing ratings, with coverage lanes, pursuit angles and CPU lane reads. Outside linebackers still use temporary ratings. DLs have individual Speed, Acceleration, Strength and Tackling ratings; their maximum speed is 78% of DB speed, versus 90% for MLBs. All player movement is slowed by 15% without changing ratings. A tied game ends tied. Teams switch ends at halftime. Esc pauses (P also works). T calls a timeout before the snap or between plays; Enter resumes it. Offense: WASD moves up/left/down/right; F slides. Jukes are deferred. Defense: WASD moves, Space intercepts, contact tackles. Tackles animate knockback; the ball is spotted at forward progress. Standard coverage gives blocks more time before shed checks than blitz pressure. Each OL has individual Blocking, Pass Protector and Run Protector ratings; better ratings lower the chance of a defender shedding. TE Speed and Acceleration cap at 95. Protection now reduces contact fumbles. Stamina is randomized from 60–99 for every player except OL and counts toward overall. Sustained running drains energy and gradually reduces speed by up to 32%; standing still recovers energy. RBs and DBs sustain speed longest, followed by WRs, TEs and MLBs at equal stamina. Energy resets between plays. A small bar under the ball carrier and selected defender shows remaining energy. Catching 80+ enables automatic dives for off-target passes; reach grows with Catching, while the timed catch/interception input still applies. Catches use high-point, reaching, over-the-shoulder, toe-tap and diving animations. Passes outside the sideline are incomplete. Toe taps establish possession inbounds before stepping out. A diving carrier is down on opponent contact, otherwise gets up after about one second. Extra-point choices can be queued during celebrations. Plays have a quiet 2.4-second delay before the next play call, with touchdown and 20-yard-play celebrations. Run blocks have a lower shed rate. Free blockers reassess threats ahead of the runner, share assignments, and lead downfield instead of chasing defenders behind the play. CPU play calls weigh down, distance, score, time, roster strengths and recent user run/pass calls. CPU QBs look for separation within their throwing range. Blockers form a pocket on pass plays and engage defenders to create run lanes; protection requires contact and ends when a defender sheds or gets past the block. The current pixel art is the team-art standard; uniforms can be recolored later.
